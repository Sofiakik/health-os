# Foundations of Advanced Jobs To Be Done

## The Core Principle

People don't buy products — they **hire** them to get a job done.

A **job** is the progress a person is trying to make in a specific situation. It's not about the product itself, but about what the customer is truly trying to accomplish and what's blocking them from doing it perfectly.

This simple reframing changes everything about how you think about your product.

## The Job Hierarchy

Jobs exist at different levels, forming a pyramid from broad aspirations down to specific actions. Understanding these levels helps you understand what your product really competes with.

### Big Job (Большая работа)
The overarching aspiration — the ultimate change a person wants to make in their life or situation.

**Example:** "Preserve money while accounting for inflation"

### Small Job (Малая работа)
Intermediate steps on the path toward the Big Job. A person may have multiple Small Jobs that together help achieve the Big Job.

**Examples:** "Buy bonds" or "Place money on deposit"

### Core Job (Основная работа)
The specific, granular job that your product solves. This is what your customers actually hire your product to do.

**Example:** "Buy reliable bonds via Yango platform"

## The Critical Insight: Competing on Jobs, Not Features

To replace a competitor, you don't win by offering a better solution to the **same job**. You win by **shifting the customer's perception of what Core Job they need done in the first place**.

This is why feature-parity rarely wins against incumbents. If you're solving the same job they're solving, they already have an established solution. But if you can reframe the job, you reframe the entire competitive landscape.

## Three Dimensions of Every Job

Every job — whether functional, emotional, or social — shapes what a customer truly needs.

### 1. Functional
The practical, measurable task that needs to be done.

**Example:** Understand my sleep quality

This is the obvious part, but it's often the least important.

### 2. Emotional
How the person wants to feel when the job is done or during the process.

**Example:** Feel in control and confident about my health

People want solutions that make them feel capable, secure, or hopeful — not just solutions that deliver a technical outcome.

### 3. Social
How the person wants to be seen by others as a result of getting this job done.

**Example:** Be perceived as someone who takes health seriously

This can be a subtle motivator, but it's often the deciding factor when two solutions are functionally equivalent.

## Types of Jobs

Not all jobs follow the same pattern.

### Sequential Jobs
Jobs that must be done in a specific order. You can't do job B until you've completed job A.

**Example:** Before you can "get mortgage approval," you must first "understand how to afford an apartment."

### Parallel Jobs
Jobs that happen independently or simultaneously. They're related but don't depend on each other.

**Example:** A student might "improve study skills" while also "maintain social relationships" — both matter, both happen at the same time, but one doesn't block the other.

## Activating Knowledge

**Activating Knowledge** is the critical insight or experience that makes a person realize a better solution exists.

Without Activating Knowledge, customers stay loyal to existing solutions — not because those solutions are perfect, but because they don't see an alternative.

When you introduce Activating Knowledge, you create a moment of realization: "Wait, there's a *better way* to do this?"

This is the moment customer switch from old solutions to new ones.

## The Yango Bond Case Study

Yango is a Russian fintech platform that became the leading bond investment platform by understanding this principle.

### The Situation
In Russia, ordinary people stored money in bank deposits. It was safe, predictable, and everyone did it.

### The Jobs
- **Big Job:** Preserve money against inflation
- **Old Core Job (what customers thought they needed):** "Put money in a bank deposit at Sberbank"
- **New Core Job (what Yango positioned):** "Buy reliable bonds"

### The Activating Knowledge
Yango didn't try to be a better deposit platform. Instead, they provided Activating Knowledge: **"Bond returns can exceed deposit rates with equivalent safety."**

This single insight changed what job customers thought they needed to do.

### The Result
By March 2021, Yango had accumulated:
- 73,000 accounts
- 25 billion rubles in assets

### The Key Lesson
Yango didn't compete on deposit features (they can't outdo Sberbank at their own game). Instead, they shifted the competitive landscape by redefining the Core Job.

They said: *You don't need to choose between safety and returns anymore. You can have both by buying bonds instead of using deposits.*

## The Central Question of JTBD

The question that should drive every product decision isn't:

> *"Who is my user and what features do they want?"*

The question is:

> *"What job is the person trying to get done, and what's stopping them from doing it perfectly?"*

Once you answer that, everything changes. Your features, your marketing, your roadmap — they all become means to getting the job done, not ends in themselves.

## Why This Matters for Product

When you think in terms of jobs, you shift from competition to insight:

- You stop asking "How can we build a better X?" and start asking "What should people actually be doing instead?"
- You understand why customers use your product AND why they abandon it
- You see opportunities where features alone can't compete
- You can differentiate by reframing the problem, not just solving it better

The job your product solves is more important than any feature it has.
