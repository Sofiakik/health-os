# B2C JTBD Interview Guide

A practical reference for conducting Jobs-to-be-Done interviews with consumer users.

---

## Overview

**Total interview time:** ~73 minutes

**Structure:**
1. Introduction (3 min)
2. Qualification (10 min)
3. Study Job Graph (47 min)
4. Previous/Following Jobs (17 min)
5. Optional: Decision-making interview (15 min)

---

## Phase 1 — Introduction (3 minutes)

**Goal:** Establish rapport and set expectations

### Script

- Thank them for their time
- Explain briefly what you'll discuss (you can mention the job hypothesis but not business details)
- Tell them you'll take notes so nothing is missed
- Warn that you'll ask about emotions — explain why it matters (emotions reveal what really motivates behavior)
- Ask permission to record
- Tell them to ask if any question is unclear or uncomfortable

---

## Phase 2 — Qualification (10 minutes)

**Goal:** Understand the respondent's context and past experience

This phase determines which path you'll take in Phase 3.

### Key Questions

- Ask qualifying questions to understand their psychological profile and relevant context
- Clarify which jobs and solutions are relevant to study for this specific person
- Determine whether they use the solution you're interested in, or have used similar ones
- Understand their experience level and how recently they've engaged with this job

### What to Listen For

- Their current approach to the job
- How long they've been doing this
- What alternatives they've tried
- Whether they're actively using a solution or still struggling

---

## Phase 3 — Study the Job Graph (47 minutes)

**Goal:** Understand the full context, motivation, and experience around how they get a specific result

### Choosing Your Path

#### PATH A — You have a job hypothesis AND know which solution they use

Start with:
- "Please tell me how you do [job hypothesis]"
- "In what situations do you usually find yourself when using [solution]?"
- "What results do you usually expect from using [solution]?"

If they mention multiple results:
- "Which is most important? Most problematic? Most frequent? What would you rank 2nd and 3rd?"

Then proceed to the full question set below, focusing on their top 1-2 results.

#### PATH B — You DON'T have a job hypothesis but know the solution they use

Start with:
- "In what situation did you find yourself when you decided to use [solution]?"
- "What results did you expect from using [solution]?"

Select 1-2 most interesting results and continue with the full question set below.

---

## Full Question Set for Studying Each Job

Work through these blocks systematically for each important job. Not every interview will hit all questions — use judgment to go deeper on what matters most.

### [Expected Result]

"What result do you usually get from using [solution]? What tasks does it help with?"

**What to listen for:** The concrete outcomes they're after. Don't settle for vague answers — push for specifics.

---

### [Expected Result Criteria]

"Please tell me in detail what criteria matter to you for [expected result]?"

Examples: accuracy, speed, convenience, cost, appearance, feeling, durability, etc.

**What to listen for:** The specific dimensions of quality that matter to them. If they mention multiple criteria, ask which is most important.

---

### [Activating Knowledge] (optional)

"What did you learn that made you start getting [expected result]? Was there a specific experience?"

**What to listen for:** Often reveals a triggering moment or education gap that motivated change.

---

### [Solution Story]

"Please tell me briefly the story of how you usually buy and use [solution] to get [expected result]?"

**What to listen for:** The full process — where they heard about it, how they decided, how they use it, what they had to learn. This reveals barriers and satisfactions.

---

### [Context]

"In what situation do you usually find yourself when you decide to use [solution] to get [expected result]?"

**What to listen for:** The repeating scenario that triggers the need. What's happening around them, internally or externally?

---

### [Trigger]

"At what moment do you usually start doing something to get [expected result]? What triggers it?"

**What to listen for:** The specific signal — time of day, event, feeling, or circumstance that prompts action. This is how the job becomes active.

---

### [Why — Job Above]

"Why do you want to get [expected result]? What is it for?"

**What to listen for:** The higher-order purpose. This often reveals they're hiring the solution for a different job than you thought. Ask "why" multiple times if needed.

---

### [Positive Emotions on Success]

"How do you want to feel after you get [expected result]? What emotion?"

**What to listen for:** The emotional reward. Examples: confident, in control, safe, proud, relieved, accomplished. Use their exact language.

---

### [Negative Emotions on Failure]

"When you don't get [expected result], do you experience negative emotions?"

**Important:** Ask about emotions BEFORE using the solution, not about problems during use. You're looking for the motivation gap they're trying to fill.

**What to listen for:** Fear, frustration, shame, anxiety, regret. Again, capture their exact language.

---

### [Frequency]

"How many times per month/year do you use [solution] to get [expected result]?"

**What to listen for:** Is this a daily habit, weekly ritual, monthly emergency, or annual need? Frequency reveals importance and habituation.

---

### [Importance]

"How important is it to get [expected result]? On a scale of 1-10, where 10 = life and death / safety of you and your family?"

If they answer 8-10:
- "Help me understand why you rated it so high?"

**What to listen for:** This anchors motivation. Scores of 8+ typically indicate jobs you should focus on. Lower scores might mean this isn't actually their job.

---

### [Satisfaction with Solution]

"On a scale of 1-10, how satisfied are you with how [solution] lets you get [expected result]? Where 10 = perfect fit, 1 = completely unsuitable?"

If they don't answer 10:
- "What are you not satisfied with?"

**What to listen for:** The gap between what they need and what they're getting. This is where opportunities live.

---

### [Price/Value]

"How much do you pay for [solution]?"

"On a scale of 1-10, how well does the price align with the value you get?"

**What to listen for:** Don't judge the price — understand if they feel they're getting what they pay for. A high price might be acceptable if the job is important enough.

---

### [Problems]

"Were there problems in the process of getting [expected result] with [solution]? Times you got stuck or couldn't get the result you wanted?"

"Were there other problems with [solution]?"

**What to listen for:** Friction points, failures, workarounds, and situations where it doesn't work. These are real constraints.

---

### [Barriers to Starting]

"Was there anything that stopped you from starting to use [solution]?"

**What to listen for:** Cost, complexity, skepticism, habit, fear of change. Understanding what almost prevented adoption reveals what needs to be overcome for new users.

---

### [Alternatives Considered]

"Did you consider other products to get [expected result]? If yes — tell me about them."

If they used a different solution in the past → conduct a Switch Interview:
- "What made you switch from [old solution] to [current solution]?"
- "At what moment did you realize [old solution] wasn't working?"
- "What almost stopped you from making the switch?"

**What to listen for:** Competitive context and why your solution won over alternatives. This reveals true job fit.

---

## Phase 4 — Previous and Following Jobs (17 minutes)

**Goal:** Understand the full job sequence and see if there are other opportunities

### [Previous Job]

"What did you do before you got [solution] to get [expected result]?"

**What to listen for:** The workaround, manual process, or alternative solution they used before. This shows job continuity and how entrenched the old way is.

---

### [Following Job]

"What do you do after using [solution] to get [high-level expected result]?"

**What to listen for:** What happens next in their workflow? Is there a follow-up job that could also be improved? Does the solution create new problems that need solving?

---

## Phase 5 — Optional: Decision-Making Interview (15 minutes)

**Use this if you have a specific offer to evaluate.**

Ask how your current offer maps to this specific respondent's job. Does your solution fit what they actually need?

### Key Questions

- "If I described a solution that [specific feature], would that help you get [expected result]?"
- "What would make that solution better than what you use now?"
- "Would you switch if [feature] existed?"
- "At what price would this be worth switching for you?"

---

## After the Interview — What to Do with the Data

### Write Up the Job Description

Create a concise job statement:
> When [context], I want to [expected result], so I can [job above]

### Record Key Metrics

- Importance score (1-10)
- Satisfaction score (1-10)
- Frequency (per day/week/month)
- Price/value alignment score

### Flag Switching Moments

Note any points where they considered switching, almost abandoned the solution, or expressed frustration. These are signals of opportunity.

### Capture Emotional Language

Exact words matter. Record the specific emotions they used:
- "What words did they use to describe success?"
- "What words did they use to describe failure?"

This language will help you communicate with future users.

### Identify Job Sequence

Note:
- The job above (why they want this result)
- Any jobs that come after
- Whether there are related jobs they mentioned

---

## Interview Tips

**Listen more than you talk.** Your job is to understand, not to pitch or explain.

**Don't rush.** Silence is okay. Give them time to think and remember.

**Ask follow-up questions.** "Tell me more about that" and "What do you mean by [word]?" are your best tools.

**Don't suggest solutions.** Never ask "Would you like a feature that..." during the exploration phase. Save that for Phase 5 if at all.

**Take notes by hand.** It's slower, which forces you to listen and select what matters. Recording is good too — take notes AND record.

**Notice what's NOT said.** If they never mention price, that might mean price isn't a barrier. If they never talk about features, that might mean features don't matter to them.

**Watch for emotion.** When their voice changes, when they light up, when they get frustrated — that's where the real job is.
