# Critical Job Chains

## Core Idea

Jobs don't exist in isolation. Every job that a client does sits inside a **chain**: the client's job is completed by someone getting paid, and that payment flows through a sequence of roles. Understanding the full chain — not just your immediate client's jobs — reveals hidden leverage points, broken links, and growth opportunities.

Jobs exist not only for clients but **for your own business** as well. Mapping the full chain shows you where your business jobs sit relative to the whole system.

---

## Chain Structures by Business Model

### B2C Product

The simplest chain:

```
Client jobs → Your business jobs
```

The client has a job to do. Your product executes it. Revenue flows from client to you.

---

### B2B Product

B2B has two layers of buyer jobs:

```
Business-заказчик (business jobs + personal jobs of ЛПР)
    → ЛВР (influencer/implementer jobs)
        → Your business jobs
```

- **ЛПР (decision-maker)**: has both business jobs (ROI, efficiency, compliance) AND personal jobs (look good to their boss, avoid risk of a bad decision, advance career)
- **ЛВР (influencer/user)**: the person who actually uses the product — their jobs also affect whether the deal closes and whether the product gets used
- Often easier to find your focus segment among ЛПРs because their jobs are more visible and tied directly to budget decisions

---

### B2B2C Product

Three-layer chain:

```
End consumer jobs
    → Intermediary (business jobs + personal ЛПР jobs)
        → Your business jobs
```

Example: A fintech tool sold to accounting firms (intermediary) who serve individuals (end consumers). You must understand what the end consumer needs the accountant to accomplish — because the accountant's willingness to pay depends on their ability to serve the end consumer better.

---

### Marketplace / Platform

```
Seller jobs → Platform jobs → Buyer jobs
```

Or multi-sided: each side has its own chain. Revenue can flow from either side or both. The segment worth targeting is usually the side whose unmet job is the biggest bottleneck to the whole chain.

---

## "Follow the Money"

**The key heuristic:** Find the segment among people whose job execution **drives cash flow** in the chain.

In any chain, some jobs are the critical value-creation moments that cause money to actually move. If someone fails to execute that job, money stops flowing. Those jobs — and the people who hold them — are your highest-leverage segments.

In B2B, the ЛПР usually controls budget, so their jobs (both business and personal) are the most important to understand.

---

## Why You Need to Map the Full Chain

Knowing the complete chain lets you:

1. **Fix bottlenecks** — identify the job in the chain that fails most often and disrupts the whole flow
2. **Find hidden roles** — discover stakeholders you didn't know existed but who can block or accelerate adoption
3. **Reduce total interactions** — each handoff in a chain is a probability of failure; fewer interactions = higher completion rate
4. **Find orphan jobs** — jobs with no clear owner and no KPI measuring their execution are usually broken and represent opportunity
5. **Optimize cycles** — jobs that repeat in loops (approval cycles, revision cycles) often have the highest optimization leverage
6. **Understand switching barriers** — the more a product is embedded in multiple chain roles, the harder it is to replace

---

## How to Prioritize Within a Chain

Not all broken links are equally worth fixing. Prioritize based on:

- **Criticality**: How often does this interaction fail? How badly does failure affect the whole chain?
- **Time delays**: Measure how long each step takes. Large delays = high friction = high value if fixed.
- **Variability**: High variance in execution time is itself a problem — it breaks planning and creates anxiety.
- **Quality variance**: Steps with inconsistent output quality create downstream problems.
- **Cycle optimization**: Jobs that repeat in loops (e.g., monthly close, weekly review) are especially high-leverage because fixing them multiplies improvement frequency.

## Case Study 1: B2B SaaS — Financial Control

**Segment found among ЛПРs of SMB companies.**

Chain: CEO/CFO (ЛПР: business job = run profitable business, personal job = sleep at night) → Bookkeeper/accountant (ЛВР: job = close the month without errors) → Your SaaS.

Core insight: The bookkeeper's Core Job is "закрыть месяц без ошибок" (close the month without errors). This is the critical link: if the bookkeeper fails, the CEO can't see accurate financials, can't make decisions, can't file taxes on time. The chain breaks.

Value proposition aimed at ЛПР: "Your bookkeeper will close the month without errors, giving you accurate financials on time." But the product must actually solve the bookkeeper's job at the execution level.

---

## Case Study 2: B2B2C — Telemedicine

Chain: End consumer (job: "получить диагноз и план лечения" — get a diagnosis and treatment plan) → Medical clinic (business job: process patients efficiently; ЛПР personal job: maintain clinic reputation and avoid malpractice) → Telemedicine platform (your business).

The critical job in the chain: the patient actually completing their treatment plan, not just receiving a diagnosis. If treatment compliance is low, the clinic's outcomes are bad, the platform gets blamed, and retention collapses.

Mapping this chain revealed that the platform needed to own not just the consultation but the follow-up job ("follow through on treatment") — which was an orphan job with no owner.

---

## How to Use This in Practice

1. **Draw the full chain** before designing your product or sales motion. Map every role from end consumer to your business.
2. **Identify the critical link** — the job whose failure most disrupts cash flow or value delivery.
3. **Find where ownership is missing** — jobs with no clear owner and no KPI are almost always poorly executed and represent high-value opportunities.
4. **Choose your entry point** based on where you can execute better than any existing solution, and where that execution most directly drives revenue in the chain.
5. **Don't get stuck at the top of the chain** — understanding end consumer jobs (even in B2B contexts) often reveals what the ЛПР actually needs to care about.
